# IRB brief — NAVI Phase 1 survey (facts only)

Use this to draft an SCSU IRB proposal. Do not invent details not listed here.

## Personnel
- Student researcher: Shreyash Parajuli, graduate student, Department of Computing, Informatics and Data Science, St. Cloud State University (shreyash.parajuli@go.stcloudstate.edu)
- Faculty advisor: Dr. Bhaskar Ghosh (bhaskar.ghosh@stcloudstate.edu)

## Study
- Title (as shown to participants): Everyday situations and language
- Type: One-time online survey on the participant’s own device
- Duration: About 10–13 minutes
- Compensation: None

## Purpose
Measure which everyday English-use situations are difficult, stressful, or avoided for recently arrived international students at SCSU. Optionally, show fixed example practice exchanges (participant input / AI-partner output with different amounts of first-language mixing) and ask rating questions about those examples.

## Participants
- SCSU students, age 18 or older
- Screening also records U.S. arrival month/year and student group (degree-seeking; degree-seeking with EAP; exchange/study abroad; Intensive English Center)
- Declining consent or failing screening ends the survey; no data are stored
- Recruitment primarily by email, with help from ISA, ESL / Intensive English programs, and student organizations

## Procedures
1. Read consent and agree or decline
2. Screening questions
3. Background: first language; self-rated English speaking, listening, reading
4. Rate the same 10 everyday situations (order randomized) on frequency, difficulty, stress, and avoidance
5. If the participant’s first language has prepared examples: show 2 situations (of clinic, professor, ordering, smalltalk); for each, show 3 fixed example exchanges labeled A/B/C (mixing levels none / light / heavy, order randomized); ask understandability, naturalness, preference, and whether they would use that style to practice
6. Questions about whether an app like this would be useful (situations to practice, typing vs talking, when, willingness to pay)
7. Coping strategies and tools currently used for English
8. Two optional open-ended questions
9. Optional: willingness to be interviewed (30–45 min), willingness to try an early version of NAVI later, and email if either is yes

Full question wording and options: see `questions.md` in the project (attach if the IRB asks for the instrument).

## Critical method constraint
The survey does **not** call an AI system. Example exchanges are fixed text prepared in advance and shown as a visual aid. Nothing the participant types is sent to an AI service.

## Answering and leaving
Participants must answer each question to continue, except items marked optional (open-ended and contact). They may leave at any time by closing the page.

## Risks and benefits (as stated to participants)
- Risks: minimal; possible mild discomfort thinking about times language was difficult
- Benefits: no direct benefit other than a short summary of their own answers at the end
- Voluntary; no effect on enrollment, grades, academic standing, or immigration/visa; not connected to offices that handle those; withdraw by closing the page

## Data
- Not collected: name, student ID, IP address
- Survey answers identified only by a random code
- Planned storage: SCSU Qualtrics; survey answers and optional contact/email in **separate** Qualtrics surveys so email is never stored with answers
- Access: researcher and faculty advisor only
- Results reported at group level only
- De-identified answers kept at least three years; may be used in future related research
- Optional email deleted when the study ends
- Data collection to Qualtrics will not be turned on until IRB approval and confirmation of the allowed storage location

## Consent
Electronic consent in the survey before any questions. Consent form text in the app must match the IRB-approved consent word for word. Protocol number is not assigned yet.
