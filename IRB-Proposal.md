## 3. Purpose of the Study

The study measures which everyday English-use situations are difficult, stressful, or avoided for recently arrived international students at St. Cloud State University.

Optionally, the survey shows fixed example practice exchanges. Each exchange is a participant input and an AI practice partner output, with different amounts of first-language mixing. Participants answer rating questions about those examples.

## 5. Procedures

### 5.1 Order of the Survey

1.  Read the consent information and agree or decline.

<!-- -->

1.  Answer the screening questions.

2.  Background: first language, and self-rated English for speaking, listening and reading.

3.  Rate the same 10 everyday situations on frequency, difficulty, stress and avoidance. The order of the situations is randomized for each participant.

4.  If the participant’s first language has prepared examples, view example practice exchanges for 2 situations and answer rating questions about them (Section 6).

5.  Answer questions about whether an app like this would be useful: situations to practice, typing or talking, when help would be useful, and willingness to pay.

6.  Answer questions about coping strategies and the tools currently used for English.

7.  Two optional open-ended questions.

8.  Optional: say whether they are willing to be interviewed for 30 to 45 minutes, and whether they are willing to try an early version of NAVI later. An email address is asked for only if either answer is yes.

### 5.2 Answering and Leaving

Participants must answer each question on a page before they can continue, except items marked optional (the two open-ended questions and the contact questions). Participants may leave the survey at any time by closing the page. Closing the page ends participation; unfinished responses are not submitted.

### 5.3 Randomization

- The order of the 10 everyday situations is randomized for each session and stored in the field situation_order.

- For the example practice exchanges, 2 of the 4 example situations are chosen at random. Within each situation, the three versions are shown in random order and labeled Version A, B and C.

- Answers about the examples are stored by mixing level (none, light, heavy), not by letter.

## 6. Example Practice Exchanges

### 6.1 Method Constraint

**The survey does not call an AI system.** The example exchanges are fixed text prepared in advance and shown as a visual aid. Nothing the participant types is sent to an AI service.

### 6.2 What Participants See

Example exchanges are shown only if the participant’s first language has prepared examples. Participants whose first language is “Other” skip this part.

Languages with prepared examples: Chinese (Mandarin), French, German, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Nepali, Russian, Spanish.

Each participant sees 2 of these 4 situations:

- Talking to a doctor or someone at a clinic

- Asking a professor a question after class

- Ordering food at a counter or a restaurant

- Making small talk with a classmate before class starts

For each situation, the participant sees the same short exchange three ways:

**Table 5. Versions of each example exchange**

| **Version**  | **Description**                                                                          |
|--------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| English only | No mixing. The same English version is shown to every language group.                    |
| Light mixing | A small part of the AI practice partner’s output is in the participant’s first language. |
| Heavy mixing | Most of the AI practice partner’s output is in the participant’s first language.         |

After the three versions, participants rate how well they understood each version, how natural each version sounds, which version would help them most, and whether they would use that style to practice.

**The full You (input) / NAVI (output) text of every exchange in every language is in Appendix C** (scroll to that appendix; it is long).

## 7. Risks

The risks are minimal. Some participants may feel mild discomfort thinking about times when language was difficult.

Participants can leave at any time by closing the page. Names, student IDs and IP addresses are not collected.

## 8. Benefits

There is no direct benefit to participants other than a short summary of their own answers shown at the end of the survey. There is no payment for taking part.

## 9. Voluntary Participation

- Participation is voluntary.

- Taking part, or not taking part, has no effect on enrollment, grades, academic standing, or immigration or visa status.

- The study is not connected to any office that handles those things.

- Participants can leave at any time by closing the page.

## 10. Data Collection, Storage and Confidentiality

### 10.1 Information Not Collected

Name, student ID and IP address are not collected.

### 10.2 Identification of Responses

Survey answers are identified only by a random code (participant_id). The code is kept in the participant’s browser so a repeat attempt from the same browser can be recognized (play_number).

### 10.3 Storage

Planned storage is SCSU Qualtrics. Survey answers and the optional contact information are stored in two separate Qualtrics surveys, so an email address is never stored with answers.

**Table 6. Separation of stored data**

| **Qualtrics survey** | **Contains**                                                          | **Does not contain**                 |
|----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Survey responses     | All survey answers and session metadata, identified by participant_id | Email, interview or beta willingness |
| Contact              | participant_id, interview_ok, beta_ok, contact_email                  | Any survey answers                   |

The submission service accepts only the field names listed in Appendix D and rejects any response without consent.

### 10.4 Access

Only the student researcher and the faculty advisor have access to the data.

### 10.5 Reporting

Results are reported at group level only, never for individuals.

### 10.6 Retention

- De-identified answers are kept for at least three years and may be used in future related research.

- Optional email addresses are deleted when the study ends.

### 10.7 Activation of Data Collection

Data collection to Qualtrics will not be turned on until IRB approval and confirmation of the allowed storage location. Until then the survey runs in test mode, and nothing leaves the participant’s browser.

## 11. Informed Consent Process

- Consent is electronic and is given in the survey before any questions.

- The participant reads the consent information and selects “Yes, I agree” or “No, I do not want to take part”. Selecting No ends the survey and nothing is stored.

- The consent text in the survey will match the IRB-approved consent form word for word.
